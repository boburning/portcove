# TechnicallyComputers PSX Linux route hold

Thirteen managed PSX recompilation records from the `TechnicallyComputers` upstream author declared a Linux x86-64 route (Bomberman Fantasy Race, Bomberman Party Edition, Bomberman World, Final Fantasy VII, Star Wars Episode I Jedi Power Battles, Klonoa, Legend of Mana, Marvel vs. Capcom, Masters of Teras Kasi, Metal Slug X, Rampage Through Time, Street Fighter Alpha 3 and Twisted Metal 4). I downloaded the real Linux ZIPs of five of them (Bomberman Fantasy Race, Rampage Through Time, Metal Slug X, Final Fantasy VII and Klonoa) and ran Rampage through Core's managed extraction. The other eight were not downloaded; they are expected to share the layout and are held on that basis. Every inspected Linux package carries the same bundled Python toolchain: 5,046 to 5,049 entries including 25 pairs of paths that differ only by letter case in the terminfo database, for example `overlay_toolchain/python/share/terminfo/2/2621A` and `2621a`, and `E/Eterm` and `e/eterm`. Core's managed extraction refuses case-colliding paths on every host to keep a package's namespace portable, and Rampage ended with "archive contains duplicate or platform-colliding path". Two Alexbeav PSX Linux packages (Ace Combat 3 and Alundra) and the Mac x64 and arm64 packages of Bomberman Fantasy Race and the Mac x64 package of Metal Slug X had no such collisions.

The fresh definitions therefore stop offering a Linux route for these thirteen. The Windows and Mac routes, and installed copies under their retained definitions, are unchanged. This change does not relax the case-collision rule, because that rule is a deliberate, host-independent safety and portability decision that deserves its own review. Reconsider when upstream ships a Linux package without case-colliding files (the colliding files are bundled Python terminfo entries, not game data), or when a reviewed Core exemption for case-sensitive hosts exists. A player can still run the upstream Linux package outside Portcove. No Linux player was executed, and no Windows package was inspected for this record.
