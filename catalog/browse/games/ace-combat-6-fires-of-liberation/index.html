Setup

  1. Use your own files, or let Portcove unpack your disc image

    Play without choosing anything: put your own Ace Combat 6 Xbox 360 disc image beside ac6recomp.exe as AC6.iso, or your own extracted game files in assets with assets/default.xex. Nothing is asked of you. Or choose your own disc image once, and Portcove checks that it is the one reviewed image, then unpacks it into the assets folder. Unpacking replaces everything in assets with the verified copy, so files you added to assets by hand are not kept when it unpacks. AC6.iso, dlc, your settings and bindings, logs and saves are never touched by unpacking. A disc image you choose is used from the next runtime update of Ace Combat 6. Only that one reviewed image, labelled USA and Japan, is accepted for unpacking; other editions and regions, including Europe, are not supported. Keep your original copy separately. Choose Play after the files are ready; this portable package has no installer. Optional own DLC belongs in dlc; title-update data is a separate upstream input, not a runtime update.

Setup notes
Save locations, settings and custom data
Saves and the default texture-mod profile use your Windows Documents/ac6recomp folder. Runtime settings and keyboard bindings sit beside the player in ac6recomp.toml and ac6_input.toml; use forward slashes in TOML paths. Runtime caches and logs also appear beside the player. If Windows cannot resolve Documents, the upstream relative ac6recomp fallback stays beside the player. Custom data, disc, DLC, log, mod and diagnostic locations remain yours to preserve separately.

Limitations and evidence

  • Only the one reviewed image is accepted for unpacking; other editions and regions, including Europe, are not supported.
  • Keep a separate backup of the Windows Documents/ac6recomp folder before changing versions. Close the game before changing runtime files; older versions may not read newer saves.
  • Upstream requires Windows 10/11 x64, AVX2 and a Direct3D 12-capable GPU. This experimental release has known performance and rendering limits; Portcove gameplay and minimum-system observations remain unqualified.
